What the data says about itself

The chart contains clues about how much confidence to place in the chart itself. Every figure in this section was recomputed from the published 2022 result table and plotting code. None of it describes the later supplied image, whose dataset remains undocumented.

  1. 01

    Not every result becomes a plotted point.

    125 →become 116

    125 result-table items → 116 plotted

    The published plot draws an item only when it has more than 10 new Nootroflix ratings. The nine items left off still appear in the result table. Leaving them off says nothing about whether they work or are safe, only that there were few new ratings to place them.

  2. 02

    More ratings do not mean more reported impact.

    Popularity and reported transformation barely move together: the rank relationship is weak and positive. High volume is not evidence quality, and reported transformation is not scientific efficacy.

  3. 03

    Less data means more uncertainty.

    For sparsely rated items the model’s interval is about 4× wider (≈3.9×) than for widely rated ones. Line length shows the width of uncertainty, not the size of any effect.

  4. 04

    More ratings can narrow an estimate. They cannot turn self-report into an experiment.

    More observations can tighten the community estimate. They do not add randomization, blinding, placebo control, representative sampling or causal inference, so more ratings are not better scientific evidence.

    • Volume ≠is not truth
    • Reported impact ≠is not efficacy
    • Precision ≠is not validity
    • Signal ≠is not recommendation

The chart does not only describe interventions. Its shape also tells us how carefully the chart itself should be read.

Two questions

Signal is not evidence. Evidence is not effect size.

The community chart and the reviewed records are most useful side by side, as long as neither is read as the other.

Different datasets and different questions can produce different-looking signals; they should not be treated as contradictory unless the claims actually match. Creatine rarely felt life-changing to raters asked about cognition, while the reviewed claim is about strength, power and repeated high-intensity effort. Those are different questions, not a disagreement.
